## LinkedIn

### Option A - project story angle

I just shipped a browser game about SDG 15: Life on Land, built with nothing but vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript (plus three.js for the 3D rendering). No game engine, no build step, no bundler.

🌍 Guardians of the Land drops you into a valley where desertification is spreading, loggers are cutting the last forest, poachers are after the wildlife, and droughts bring wildfires. You plant, irrigate, and defend your way to a restored ecosystem, while a live simulation runs underneath. Every cell of land has a health value, trees heal the ground they're planted on, and if you neglect it the desert genuinely creeps outward.

A few things I'm proud of:
🌲 A real day/night cycle with a shader-driven sky and moving sunlight
🔊 Fully procedural audio. Every sound is synthesized at runtime with the Web Audio API, zero audio files shipped
🦌 Wildlife that only shows up once there's forest to support it, and flees as habitat degrades
🏆 A local leaderboard, a Ranger Skills upgrade tree, and colorblind-safe / high-contrast accessibility modes

SDG 15 calls for protecting and restoring terrestrial ecosystems and combating desertification. This game doesn't simulate policy, it tries to simulate the feeling of the goal: restoration is slow, degradation is fast if you look away, and defending what you've already rebuilt matters as much as planting something new.

I built Guardians of the Land to find out. A 3D game about SDG 15 (Life on Land), running entirely on vanilla JS and three.js. No engine, no build step, just index.html and a static server.

Plant trees, irrigate dying land, fight off loggers and poachers, and race the desert before it swallows the valley. Under the hood it's a live health-grid simulation, nothing is scripted, the land actually degrades and recovers cell by cell.
